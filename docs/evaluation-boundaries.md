# Evaluation boundaries

## What is evaluated

The public benchmark asks whether a skill helps a language model produce advice or revisions that:

- expose a coherent publication thesis and contribution;
- trace claims to exact evidence and scope;
- preserve inference strength, constructs, units, paradigms, nulls, harms, and author stance;
- distinguish human, model, material, and institutional labor;
- avoid invented citations and unverified venue rules;
- give an author specific, usable next actions.

## What is not evaluated

- conference acceptance probability;
- the scientific truth of evidence not present in a case packet;
- plagiarism, authorship, or an “AI-written percentage”;
- performance on every discipline, venue, language, manuscript, or model;
- whether an author has fulfilled their own accountability and disclosure obligations.

The final phrase “higher on the frozen benchmark” means exactly: the 1.2 candidate exceeded the
captured ClaudeEssaySkill condition by 3.3125 points on Publication-Craft-12 v3, above a predeclared
two-point margin, without more confirmed hard failures. It does not license the shorter and broader
claim that one skill is simply “better at papers.”

Automatic style indicators can point to repetitive, defensive, vague, or templated passages. They
cannot identify who wrote a passage. A clean style audit cannot rescue a weak contribution, and a
flagged passage is not proof of machine authorship.

## Why the private/public split exists

The private core keeps the executable skill, scripts, paper-derived routing cases, adversarial cases,
hidden manifests, raw answers, judge files, and candidate mapping. The public demonstration exposes
the method, frozen rubric, aggregate results, synthetic before/after examples, and charts. This split
preserves the evaluation firewall and prevents the public repository from becoming an answer key.
